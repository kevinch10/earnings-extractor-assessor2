LTC REPORTS 2026 THIRD QUARTER RESULTS AS SHOP MOMENTUM CONTINUES

​

– Year-to-Date SHOP Acquisitions of Nearly $950 Million Surpass Previously Disclosed Mid-Point Target –

– SHOP Now Represents 45% of Company’s Annualized Net Operating Income –

​

​

WESTLAKE VILLAGE, CALIFORNIA, November 3, 2026 -- LTC Properties, Inc. (NYSE: LTC) (“LTC” or the “Company”), a real estate investment trust specializing in seniors housing and health care properties, today announced operating results for the third quarter ended September 30, 2026.

“Our SHOP strategy is delivering and reshaping LTC’s future by transforming our portfolio, building a scalable operating platform, and strengthening our ability to capitalize on new growth opportunities,” said Clint Malin, LTC’s Co-CEO. “SHOP acquisitions have reached nearly $950 million through October, surpassing our 2026 mid-point acquisition target, increasing our earnings power, and extending our runway for growth. As SHOP contributes a greater share of net operating income, we are creating meaningful long-term value for shareholders, and remain confident in our ability to further differentiate LTC as a growth-oriented seniors housing REIT.”

Summary of Financial Results:

●Total revenues: Increased $46 million primarily due to SHOP acquisitions and conversions, a lease termination fee received in the 2026 third quarter related to the sale of the leased properties, the turnaround effect of a prior year non-cash write off of straight-line effective interest, an increase in interest income from loan originations and construction funding, and higher rent from market-based rent resets and escalations. This increase was offset by decreases from asset sales and loan payoffs.
●Net income available to common stockholders: Increased $367 million primarily due to an increase in gain on sale, the turnaround effect of a prior year non-cash write off of straight-line effective interest as noted above, an increase in SHOP NOI, the lease termination fee discussed above, increases in interest income and rental income, as noted above, partially offset by an increase in depreciation and amortization and interest expenses, a 2026 third quarter impairment charge, and decreases from asset sales and loan payoffs.
●Diluted Core FFO per share: Increased $0.02 primarily due to higher income from SHOP acquisitions, an increase in interest income from loan originations and additional loan funding, and higher rental income from market-based rent resets, partially offset by lower revenue from asset sales and loan payoffs, and an increase in interest expense.
●Diluted Core FAD per share: Increased $0.01 primarily due to the same factors described above, and higher rental income from rent escalations, partially offset by recurring capital expenditures related to the Company’s SHOP assets.

Seniors Housing Operating Portfolio (“SHOP”) as of November 3, 2026

Since launching its SHOP platform in May 2025, LTC has grown the portfolio to 50 communities managed by 14 operators, 12 of which are new LTC relationships.

​

●Annualized NOI contribution: 45%.
●Acquisitions: $1.3 billion.
●Percentage of gross investments: 53%.
●Average property vintage: 9 years.
●Markets: 60% located in primary markets as designated by the National Investment Center for Seniors Housing & Care (NIC).

​

“When we introduced our SHOP platform, we envisioned creating a differentiated growth engine that would enhance LTC’s earnings profile, strengthen operator relationships, and position the company for future growth. The progress we have achieved reflects both the strength of that vision and our team’s ability to execute it,” said Pam Kessler, LTC’s Co-CEO. “While our goals were ambitious, we have successfully built a scalable platform that is generating considerable momentum. As a result, we believe LTC is better positioned to drive sustainable earnings growth, increase portfolio resilience, and build ongoing shareholder value.”

About LTC

LTC is a real estate investment trust (REIT) focused on seniors housing and health care properties, principally investing through SHOP, triple-net leases, joint ventures, and structured finance solutions. The Company’s portfolio includes nearly 170 properties throughout the United States. Based on gross real estate investments, 80% of the Company’s assets are seniors housing communities, with the remainder skilled nursing centers. Learn more at www.LTCreit.com.

7

Supplemental Reporting Measures

Funds from operations (“FFO”), funds available for distribution (“FAD”), and net operating income (“NOI”) are supplemental measures of a real estate investment trust’s (“REIT”) financial performance that are not defined by U.S. generally accepted accounting principles (“GAAP”), and are generally used by investors, analysts, and the Company. LTC believes FFO, FAD, and NOI are helpful in evaluating the operating performance of a REIT.

Real estate values historically rise and fall with market conditions, but cost accounting for real estate assets in accordance with GAAP assumes that the value of real estate assets diminishes predictably over time. LTC believes that by excluding the effect of historical cost depreciation, FFO and FAD help facilitate meaningful comparisons of operating performance between periods. LTC believes FFO is useful to reflect the operating efficiency of the Company and provide a real estate industry baseline for comparison among REITs. FAD is useful in analyzing the portion of cash flow available for distribution to stockholders. Investors, analysts, and the Company utilize FAD as an indicator of common dividend potential. The FAD payout ratio, which represents annual distributions to common shareholders expressed as a percentage of FAD, facilitates the comparison of dividend coverage among REITs.

LTC calculates and reports FFO in accordance with the definition and interpretive guidelines issued by the National Association of Real Estate Investment Trusts (“Nareit”). FFO, as defined by Nareit, means net income (loss) available to common stockholders (computed in accordance with GAAP), excluding the impact of real estate depreciation and amortization, gains or losses on the sale of real estate, impairment write-downs of depreciable real estate, and the Company’s related share of unconsolidated partnerships and joint ventures. The Company’s computation of FFO may not be comparable to FFO reported by other REITs that do not define the term in accordance with the current Nareit definition or have a different interpretation of the current Nareit definition from that of the Company. Therefore, caution should be exercised when comparing the Company’s FFO to that of other REITs.

The Company defines FAD as FFO excluding the impact of straight-line rent, amortization of lease incentives, other non-cash contra-revenue, effective interest income, non-cash compensation charges, provision for (recovery of) credit losses, and recurring capital expenditures required to maintain and re-tenant our properties.

As applicable, the Company excludes the impact of non-core adjustments on FFO and FAD, defined as Core FFO and Core FAD, respectively. These adjustments allow investors, analysts, and management to compare the Company’s operating performance on a consistent basis, independent of differences caused by unanticipated, non-operating, or non-comparable items. A detailed reconciliation of these non-core adjustments is provided in a dedicated table herein.

The Company defines NOI as net income (loss) available to common stockholders (computed in accordance with GAAP) excluding the impact of general and administrative expenses, transaction costs, write-off of effective interest, provision for (recovery of) credit losses, impairment loss, depreciation and amortization, interest expense, gain or loss on sale of real estate, and income tax provision (benefit). LTC uses NOI to reflect the operating performance of its portfolio because NOI excludes certain items that are not associated with the operations of its properties. NOI is not equivalent to LTC’s net income (loss) available to common stockholders as determined under GAAP. Additionally, the Company’s use of the term NOI may not be comparable to that of other real estate companies as they may have different methodologies for computing NOI. Therefore, caution should be exercised when comparing LTC’s NOI to that of other REITs.

While the Company uses FFO, FAD, and NOI as supplemental performance measures of the cash flow generated by operations and cash available for distribution to stockholders, such measures are not representative of cash generated from operating activities in accordance with GAAP and are not necessarily indicative of cash available to fund cash needs. These measures should not be considered an alternative to net income (loss) available to common stockholders.
