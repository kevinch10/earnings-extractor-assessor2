COASTAL FINANCIAL CORPORATION ANNOUNCES THIRD QUARTER 2026 RESULTS

Coastal Financial Corporation Reports Strong Third Quarter 2026 Earnings Driven by CCBX Program Growth
Everett, WA : October 29, 2026

Coastal Financial Corporation (Nasdaq: CCB) (the “Company”, "Coastal", "we", "our", or "us"), the holding company for Coastal Community Bank (the “Bank”), through which it operates a community-focused bank segment ("community bank") with an industry leading banking as a service ("BaaS") segment ("CCBX"), today reported unaudited financial results for the quarter ended September 30, 2026, including net income of $13.8 million, or $0.89 per diluted common share, compared to net loss of $(42.1) million, or $(2.76) per diluted common share, for the three months ended June 30, 2026 and $13.6 million, or $0.88 per diluted common share, for the three months ended September 30, 2025.

Management Discussion of the Third Quarter Results

“The third quarter better reflects the underlying earnings power of Coastal’s business, following a challenging second quarter for our shareholders. During the quarter we updated our assessment of the affected relationship, including loan portfolio performance, expected credit losses and our assessment of the collectability of amounts due under the partner's indemnification arrangement. The results of that review substantially supported the estimates established at June 30, 2026. I am pleased with the momentum we saw across the segments,” said Eric Sprink, Chief Executive Officer of Coastal.

"Beyond this relationship, we returned to profitability with net income of $13.8 million and diluted earnings per share of $0.89, and our ROA was 1.01%. BaaS program fee income grew to $14.1 million, up $2.1 million, or 17.6%, from second quarter, and $6.3 million, or 79.6%, from the same quarter last year, reflecting the strength and stability of our broader partner portfolio. Our off-balance sheet credit card accounts now stand at 1.3 million, which is approximately 236% more than where we ended the third quarter of 2025, and eight of our active partner programs are in various stages of expanding into additional products. On the community bank side, deposits grew $63.2 million, or 4.0%, to $1.64 billion in the quarter, at a cost of deposits of just 1.54%.

"We ended the quarter well capitalized, with strong cash reserves and a strong pipeline. I believe the fundamentals of this business remain strong, with solid fee income growth, robust partner activity and continued capital strength, and that we are well-positioned to continue executing on our long-term strategy".

Key Points for Third Quarter

•CCBX Growth, Partner and Product Diversification. As of September 30, 2026 we had one partner in testing, two in implementation/onboarding, and three signed letters of intent (LOIs). Our active pipeline positions us for continued growth, with new partnership opportunities and product launches expected for the remainder of 2026. Total BaaS program fee income was $14.1 million for the three months ended September 30, 2026, an increase of $2.1 million, or 17.6%, from the three months ended June 30, 2026.

As previously announced, one of our partners, Bluevine, Inc. ("Bluevine") has entered into a definitive agreement to be acquired by Valley National Bancorp ("Valley"), subject to regulatory and other customary approvals.Valley has indicated it expects the transaction to close in the second quarter of 2027, at which time the Bluevine relationship is expected to terminate, with associated deposits anticipated to transition out. At September 30, 2026, the Bank held $452.4 million in Bluevine deposits on its balance sheet and swept $1.85 billion off-balance sheet to other banks. The Bank held $3.0 million in Bluevine originated loans at quarter-end. Separately, the Bank held a $6.8 million commercial loan to Bluevine at quarter-end. Bluevine is part of a broader, diversified CCBX deposit platform. Coastal expects to replace related on-balance sheet funding through its existing sweep capabilities and

•Partner Credit Performance Stabilizing. Following the $22.8 million specific provision for credit losses and $46.0 million credit enhancement asset valuation adjustment recorded in the second quarter of 2026 related to one partner ("The Partner"), we updated our assessment of the loan portfolio performance, expected credit losses and the collectability of amounts due under the indemnification arrangement as of September 30, 2026.

Overall, the loan portfolio performance remained generally consistent with the expectations underlying the June 30 assessment, although actual charge-offs were modestly higher than previously projected. Additional provision expense recognized during the third quarter was driven in part by these higher charge-offs, as well as new loan originations and normal updates to assumptions used in the Company's allowance for credit losses methodology.

Based on management's assessment as of September 30, 2026, projected cash flows under the Partner's program agreement continued to support our accounting treatment to record substantially all of the corresponding incremental credit enhancement amounts. Approximately $3.1 million of additional provision expense was not offset by credit enhancement income because collection of that portion was not considered probable. The $22.8 million specific allowance remained unchanged, while the valuation allowance against the credit enhancement asset decreased modestly to $45.6 million from $46.0 million at June 30, 2026.

Taken together, the results of management's updated assessment substantially supported the loss estimates established in the second quarter. Management continues to closely monitor the Partner's credit performance and its ability to satisfy its contractual obligations and will update its estimates as additional information becomes available.

•Positive On- and Off-Balance Sheet Trends Continue. At September 30, 2026 we swept off-balance sheet $6.29 billion in deposits for Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation ("FDIC") insurance and liquidity purposes, and generated $1.4 million in noninterest income during the quarter ended September 30, 2026, an increase of $193,000, or 16.4%, from $1.2 million for the quarter ended June 30, 2026 and an increase of $1.1 million, or 340.5%, from $311,000 for the quarter ended September 30, 2025.

During the third quarter of 2026, we sold $5.99 billion of loans, including $4.69 billion of additional credit card receivables originated through ongoing cardholder spend and revolving activity and sold under existing forward flow arrangements, compared to $4.56 billion of sold loans in the quarter ended June 30, 2026, including $3.68 billion sold under the same forward flow arrangements. As of September 30, 2026 there were 1.3 million off-balance sheet credit cards with fee earning potential, an increase of 452,984, or 51.38%, compared to the quarter ended June 30, 2026 and an increase of 937,831, or 236.34%, from September 30, 2025.

Nonperforming assets decreased $14.7 million quarter over quarter driven by improvements across both the community bank and CCBX portfolios. The decline in CCBX nonperforming assets is consistent with the stabilization in partner credit performance and net charge-offs trending down during the quarter.

•Noninterest Expense Declined Significantly Quarter Over Quarter. Total noninterest expense decreased $56.3 million to $84.8 million in the third quarter of 2026 from $141.1 million in the second quarter. The decline primarily reflected the absence of the second quarter’s $46.0 million credit enhancement receivable valuation adjustment related to The Partner, a $5.9 million reduction in data processing and software license expenses, reflecting the absence of $4.4 million in second-quarter accelerated depreciation and lower current-quarter amortization, a $4.7 million reduction in BaaS loan expense and a $1.6 million reduction in salaries and employee benefits, comprised of $1.3 million in lower salaries and $290,000 in lower severance expense. These reductions were partially offset by a $1.6 million increase in legal and professional fees, partly related to The Partner, and a $440,000 increase in FDIC deposit insurance assessments. The latter reflected a second-quarter assessment true-up and a higher assessment rate effective in the third quarter of 2026, partly attributable to the second-quarter net loss’s effect on the FDIC assessment rate calculation.

Separately, noninterest income was reduced by $477,000 in unrealized gain on equity securities, reflecting a $565,000 downward valuation adjustment on a strategic equity investment.

•Expense Discipline and Stronger Operating Leverage: Coastal prioritizes operational efficiency and greater operating leverage. After investing significantly in people, technology and risk infrastructure to build a scaled, regulated platform, the Company is shifting its focus to achieving more consistent profitability. Management is actively managing operating expenses and has launched targeted initiatives to lower noninterest expense, with further progress expected as technology modernization matures and operational efficiencies materialize. These efforts include reviewing vendors, contractors and discretionary spending; eliminating duplication; replacing legacy systems with more efficient technology; and applying clear risk-adjusted return standards to every partner program and product. The Company is also using AI to improve employee effectiveness and productivity and reduce future costs. We have identified up to $20.0 million in potential annualized savings in noninterest expenses and expect to gradually achieve these potential savings over the next 4–6 quarters. These expenses exclude BaaS loan and fraud expenses, which are generally reimbursed by our CCBX partners and recognized in noninterest income in accordance with GAAP.

Lastly, and as part of the expense discipline discussed above, Eric Sprink, in his role as Chief Executive Officer, voluntarily agreed to a nearly 43% reduction in his Base Salary from $874,000 to $500,000 effective immediately.

•Leadership Updates: As previously announced, Joel Edwards, who served as Coastal’s Chief Financial Officer until his retirement in 2025, returned as interim Chief Financial Officer following the departure of Brandon Soto in August 2026. The Company's search for a permanent Chief Financial Officer is ongoing and in its final stages. In addition, Brian Hamilton has stepped down as President of Coastal, having led the modernization of CCBX's technology platform, including integral investments in CCBX’s infrastructure that have positioned the business for its next phase. Mr. Hamilton will remain on the Company's Board of Directors and as an advisor to the Company to support continuity across CCBX.

Capital and Liquidity

The Company and the Bank remained well capitalized at September 30, 2026, with a Company common equity Tier 1 ratio of 11.47%, a Tier 1 leverage ratio of 8.81%, and a total risk-based capital ratio of 13.82%, each above the levels required to be considered well capitalized. The Company retained $803.6 million of cash and cash equivalents and $827.4 million of additional contingent borrowing capacity, with no borrowings outstanding as of September 30, 2026. The Company’s capital efficient model, including ongoing loan sales and off-balance sheet deposit and card programs, continues to support internal capital generation.

Coastal Financial Corporation Overview
The Company has one main subsidiary, the Bank, which consists of three segments: CCBX, the community bank and treasury & administration. The CCBX segment includes all of our BaaS activities, the community bank segment includes all community banking activities and the treasury & administration segment includes treasury management, overall administration and all other aspects of the Company.  

CCBX Performance Update
Our CCBX segment continues to evolve, and we have 31 relationships, at varying stages, including one partner in testing, two in implementation/onboarding, three signed LOIs and four winding down as of September 30, 2026. We continue to focus on the composition of our partner portfolio by emphasizing relationships with well-established organizations that align with our long-term strategy, while continually evaluating our portfolio to ensure it reflects our risk and return objectives. As part of this disciplined approach, we remain focused on strengthening existing partnerships, thoughtfully expanding product offerings, winding down less promising relationships and selectively adding new relationships that complement our platform and support sustainable growth.

Our strategy is centered on building long-term partnerships and leveraging the scale of our existing relationships to drive continued growth while maintaining a strong focus on prudent risk management and operational excellence.

Increasing partner activity and transaction volumes will drive growth in noninterest income, a trend we expect to continue as existing products scale and new offerings are introduced. As part of our strategy to manage partner and lending limits, as well as overall portfolio composition and credit quality, we plan to continue selling loans. We also retain a portion of the fee income associated with processing transactions on sold credit card loans. This revenue stream continues to grow and is expected to provide ongoing income without adding balance sheet risk or capital requirements.

As our deposit base grows, we expect to continue moving deposits on and off the balance sheet, subject to applicable agreements, to manage liquidity, FDIC insurance coverage and deposit program operations. This deposit sweep capability allows us to better manage liquidity and deposit programs. At September 30, 2026 we swept off-balance sheet $6.29 billion in deposits for FDIC insurance and liquidity purposes, and generated $1.4 million in noninterest income during the quarter ended September 30, 2026, compared to $1.2 million for the quarter ended June 30, 2026. During the quarter ended September 30, 2026, eight partner programs were in various stages of expansion to include additional products, such as lines of credit, deposit programs, asset backed credit cards, credit cards and other lending products. The expansion of these and other partner initiatives is expected to drive higher partner revenue in upcoming periods.

CCBX – BaaS Reporting Information
During the quarter ended September 30, 2026, $41.8 million was recorded in BaaS credit enhancements related to the provision for credit losses - loans and reserve for unfunded commitments for CCBX partner loans and negative deposit accounts. Agreements with our CCBX partners provide a credit enhancement under which the partner indemnifies or reimburses the Bank for covered credit losses on loans, unfunded commitments and negative deposit accounts. In accordance with U.S. GAAP, we estimate expected credit losses on these exposures and record the related provision for credit losses and reserve for unfunded commitments. Concurrently, a credit enhancement asset is recognized through noninterest income (BaaS credit enhancements) representing the expected reimbursement from the partner. The collectability of the credit enhancement asset is evaluated each reporting period and a valuation adjustment is recorded when collection of all or a portion of the asset is no longer considered probable. The credit enhancement asset is relieved as credit enhancement payments and recoveries are received from the CCBX partner or taken from the partner's cash reserve account. Management regularly evaluates and manages counterparty risk associated with its CCBX partners, as the Bank could incur additional credit losses to the extent a partner is unable to fulfill its contractual obligations.
Agreements with our CCBX partners also provide protection to the Bank from fraud by indemnifying or reimbursing covered fraud losses. BaaS fraud includes non-credit fraud losses on loans and deposits originated through partners. Generally fraud losses related to loans are comprised primarily of first payment defaults. Fraud losses are recorded when incurred as losses in noninterest expense, and the enhancement received from the CCBX partner is recorded in noninterest income, resulting in a net impact of zero to the income statement.
Many CCBX partners also pledge a cash reserve account at the Bank, which the Bank can collect from when losses occur that is then replenished by the partner on a regular interval. Although agreements with our CCBX partners provide for credit enhancements that provide protection to the Bank from credit and fraud losses if our partner is unable to fulfill their contractual obligation and if the cash flows on the loans were not sufficient to fund the reimbursement of loan losses, then the Bank would be exposed to additional loan and deposit losses as a result of this counterparty risk. If a CCBX partner does not replenish their cash reserve account, the Bank may consider an alternative plan for funding the cash reserve. This may involve the possibility of adjusting the funding amounts or timelines to better align with the partner's specific situation. If a mutually agreeable funding plan is not agreed to, the Bank could declare the agreement in default, take over servicing and cease paying the partner for servicing the loan and providing credit enhancements. In the event of a partner default, the Bank would evaluate any remaining credit enhancement asset associated with that partner to determine whether a write-off is appropriate. If a write-off occurs, the Bank would stop payments to the CCBX partner and retain the full yield and any fee income on the loan portfolio going forward, decreasing our BaaS loan expense.
The Bank records contractual interest earned from the borrowers on CCBX partner loans in interest income, adjusted for origination costs, which are paid or payable to the CCBX partners. BaaS loan expense represents the amount paid or payable to partners for credit and fraud enhancements and originating & servicing CCBX loans. To determine net revenue (Net BaaS loan income) earned from CCBX loan relationships, the Bank takes BaaS loan interest income and deducts BaaS loan expense to arrive at Net BaaS loan income (a reconciliation of the non-GAAP measures are set forth in the preceding section of this earnings release) which can be compared to interest income on the Company’s community bank loans.
