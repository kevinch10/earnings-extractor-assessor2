SM Energy Reports Third Quarter 2026 Results
Operating cash flow of $1.1 billion funds $417 million of debt redemption and $133 million of capital returned to stockholders

DENVER, Nov. 3, 2026 - SM Energy Company (the “Company” or “SM”) (NYSE: SM) today reported financial and operating results for the third quarter 2026. Investor materials, including accompanying slides, can be accessed at www.sm-energy.com/investors. A conference call is scheduled for 8 a.m. MT/10 a.m. ET on November 4, 2026. Participation details are included in this release.
SM continues to execute against its three strategic priorities: Integrate, Execute and Bolster. In the third quarter, the Company substantially completed its Civitas merger (the “Merger”) integration and exceeded targeted run-rate synergies, transitioning the business to continuous improvement.
Third quarter 2026 performance is summarized below:
Integrate –
•Actioned approximately $400 million of Merger run-rate synergies – twice the original $200 million target and $25 million greater than the revised $375 million target. NPV-10 of merger synergies increased to $2.0 billion from $1.8 billion.
Execute –
•Net income was $93 million, or $0.39 per diluted share; adjusted net income1 was $465 million, or $1.95 per diluted share.1
•Generated operating cash flow of $1.1 billion; capital expenditures totaled $711 million, or $747 million before changes in accruals.1
•Delivered adjusted free cash flow1 of $397 million.
•Adjusted EBITDAX1 was $1.3 billion.
•Average net daily production totaled 433 MBoe/d, including 230 MBbl/d of oil.
•Narrowed full-year 2026 capital guidance to approximately $2.75 billion.
Bolster –
•Returned $133 million of capital to stockholders, or approximately 34% of adjusted free cash flow,1 through $81 million in share repurchases (2.3 million shares) and SM’s $0.22 per share quarterly dividend.
•Redeemed all $417 million aggregate principal amount of 6.625% Senior Notes due 2027 at par, resulting in no maturities until mid-2028.
•Reduced net debt1 to $6.0 billion, down from $7.4 billion at March 31, 2026.
1 Adjusted net income and adjusted net income per diluted share; capital expenditures before changes in accruals; adjusted free cash flow; adjusted EBITDAX; and net debt are non-GAAP measures. Refer to “Definitions of Non-GAAP Measures and Metrics as Calculated by the Company” and the accompanying reconciliations later in this release.

SM Energy President and CEO Beth McDonald said, “We are meaningfully transforming SM Energy – exceeding our merger synergy target, substantially achieving our divestiture target and materially strengthening the balance sheet. In the third quarter, we generated $1.1 billion of operating cash flow, redeemed $417 million of debt and returned $133 million to stockholders. We are modestly lowering full-year oil guidance to 220-222 MBbl/d due to transitory factors, while reiterating our full-year total production outlook. Importantly, despite service-cost inflation, we now expect 2026 capital of approximately $2.75 billion, the midpoint of our prior range, supported by synergy capture and ongoing efficiency gains. We expect the factors leading to our lower oil production outlook to be resolved in early 2027.
“This is a multi-year journey, and our strong and resilient balance sheet is the foundation. We remain on track for low one-times leverage at mid-cycle prices by year-end 2026, strengthening the capacity for durable stockholder returns. With integration substantially complete, our focus is on disciplined execution, continuous improvement and increasing the capital efficiency of the portfolio to compound long-term value for stockholders.”
Third Quarter 2026 Review
•Total production was 433 MBoe/d, including 230 MBbl/d of oil. Oil volumes were impacted by higher-than-planned downtime in the Permian Basin, related to compliance and safety work on assets acquired in the Merger and the temporary shut-in of wells during simultaneous operations, which reduced oil volumes by approximately 2 MBbl/d. We also experienced approximately 4 MBbl/d of takeaway constraints in the Uinta Basin. The Permian factors that affected third-quarter volumes have been addressed, and we expect the Uinta takeaway constraints to be resolved in early 2027.
•Net income was $0.39 per diluted share, which includes a $477 million non-cash loss from changes in the fair value of the Company’s commodity derivative contracts. Income tax expense of $30 million included current tax expense of $37 million, reflecting higher realized oil prices.
•During the third quarter, SM repurchased 2.3 million shares for $81 million and paid a $0.22 per share quarterly dividend, resulting in approximately 34% of adjusted free cash flow1 being returned to stockholders. Year-to-date share repurchases total $165 million.
Guidance
•SM lowered its full-year 2026 oil production guidance by 1% to 220-222 MBbl/d, primarily related to reasons outlined above. For full-year 2026, total production guidance was narrowed to 420-422 MBoe/d. Full-year capital expenditures are expected to be approximately $2.75 billion, or the midpoint of the prior guidance range.
•Fourth quarter 2026 production is expected to be 435-445 MBoe/d, including 229-239 MBbl/d of oil, with capital expenditures of approximately $610 million.
•Included in our oil volume guidance is a continued impact from takeaway constraints in the Uinta Basin.
•Full-year 2026 cash tax guidance is updated to approximately $100-$120 million.

DEFINITIONS OF NON-GAAP MEASURES AND METRICS AS CALCULATED BY THE COMPANY
To supplement the presentation of its financial results prepared in accordance with U.S. generally accepted accounting principles (GAAP), the Company provides certain non-GAAP measures and metrics, which are used by management and the investment community to assess the Company’s financial condition, results of operations, and cash flows, as well as compare performance from period to period and across the Company’s peer group. The Company believes these measures and metrics are widely used by the investment community, including investors, research analysts and others, to evaluate and compare recurring financial results among upstream oil and gas companies in making investment decisions or recommendations. These measures and metrics, as presented, may have differing calculations among companies and investment professionals and may not be directly comparable to the same measures and metrics provided by others. A non-GAAP measure should not be considered in isolation or as a substitute for the most directly comparable GAAP measure or any other measure of a company’s financial or operating performance presented in accordance with GAAP. Reconciliations of the Company’s non-GAAP measures to the most directly comparable GAAP measures are presented below. These measures may not be comparable to similarly titled measures of other companies.
Adjusted EBITDAX: Adjusted EBITDAX represents net income (loss) before interest expense, interest income, income taxes, depletion, depreciation, and amortization expense, exploration expense, property abandonment and impairment expense, non-cash stock-based compensation expense, derivative gains and losses net of settlements, gains and losses on divestitures, gains and losses on extinguishment of debt, non-recurring or one-time costs including transaction and integration costs associated with the Merger, and certain other items. Adjusted EBITDAX excludes certain items that we believe affect the comparability of operating results and can exclude items that are generally non-recurring in nature or whose timing and/or amount cannot be reasonably estimated. Adjusted EBITDAX is a non-GAAP measure that the Company believes provides useful additional information to investors and analysts, as a performance measure, for analysis of the Company’s ability to internally generate funds for exploration, development, acquisitions, and to service debt. The Company is also subject to financial covenants under the Company’s Credit Agreement, a material source of liquidity for the Company, based on Adjusted EBITDAX ratios. Please reference the Company’s third quarter 2026 Form 10-Q and the most recent Annual Report on Form 10-K for discussion of the Credit Agreement and its covenants.
Adjusted free cash flow: Adjusted free cash flow is calculated as net cash provided by operating activities before net change in working capital, including change in certain long-term items, less capital expenditures before changes in accruals. The Company uses this measure to represent the cash generated from operations, in excess of capital expenditures, that is available to fund discretionary uses such as debt reduction, stockholder returns, or expanding the business.
Adjusted net income and Adjusted net income per diluted common share: Adjusted net income and Adjusted net income per diluted common share exclude certain items that the Company believes affect the comparability of operating results, including items that are generally non-recurring in nature or whose timing and/or amount cannot be reasonably estimated. These items include non-cash and other adjustments, such as derivative gains and losses net of settlements, impairments, gains and losses on divestitures, gains and losses on extinguishment of debt, non-recurring or one-time costs including transaction and integration costs associated with the Merger, and accruals for non-recurring matters. The Company uses these measures to evaluate the comparability of the Company's ongoing operational results and trends and believes these measures provide useful information to investors for analysis of the Company's fundamental business on a recurring basis.
Net debt: Net debt is calculated as the total principal amount of outstanding senior     notes plus amounts drawn on the revolving credit facility less cash and cash equivalents (also referred to as total funded debt). The Company uses net debt as a measure of financial position and believes this measure provides useful additional information to investors to evaluate the Company’s capital structure and financial leverage.
Capital expenditures: The Company’s operating plan guidance uses the term “capital expenditures,” which is defined to be before changes in accruals (excludes working capital), and is a non-GAAP measure. In reliance on the exception provided by Item 10(e)(1)(i)(B) of Regulation S-K, the Company is unable to provide a reconciliation of forward-looking non-GAAP capital expenditures because components of the calculations are inherently unpredictable, such as changes to, and the timing of, capital accruals, unknown future events, and estimating certain future GAAP measures. The inability to project certain components of the calculation could significantly affect the accuracy of a reconciliation.
