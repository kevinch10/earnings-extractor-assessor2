“Our identified pipeline grew more than 30 percent compared to last quarter, and we have secured the critical contract positions needed to compete for significant awards in 2027 and beyond, with IDIQ awards already converting into funded task orders,” said Dirk Wallinger, CEO of York. “Following the election, we expect greater demand for competition and vendor diversity and a significant realignment of government resources across proven providers. Expansion into adjacent markets further broadens our addressable market, building on the technology, production capacity, and infrastructure we’ve already invested in.”



Third Quarter 2026 Company Results
Revenue for the quarter was $84.2 million, down $6.6 million, or 7% year over year.

Gross Margin was 18% in the current year quarter, down 400 bps from last year’s Q3; Gross Profit was $15.5 million, down from $20.3 million in the year-ago quarter, primarily driven by lower revenues in the quarter.

York recorded a non-cash goodwill impairment charge of $749.7 million, as a result of the difficulty in estimating with sufficient certainty the timing and amount of future contract awards beyond 2026.

Backlog stood at $581.0 million on September 30, down slightly in the third quarter. Our backlog increased by four new contract wins and modifications of $17 million, as well as increases coming from York subsidiaries of $44 million.
Selected Third Quarter and Recent Highlights
•York secured two task orders supporting the U.S. Space Force’s Space Data Network (SDN), advancing multi-vendor interoperability demonstrations and Space Exchange Point development. The awards build on York’s SDN contract position and demonstrate the opportunity to apply its spacecraft payload integration and ground infrastructure capabilities as the government develops scalable, interoperate space communications architectures.

•York was selected for the U.S. Space Force’s NITE-STAR IDIQ contract, expanding its opportunity to support space test and training missions. The contract positions York to compete for task orders combining its spacecraft platforms with ATLAS’s global ground network to deliver integrated capabilities that help Space Force operators train against real-world threats.

•York introduced LX/V-CLASS, a spacecraft platform purpose-built for Very Low Earth Orbit (VLEO), to address growing government and commercial demand for higher-fidelity observation and resilient orbit diversity in VLEO, one of the fastest-growing operational domains in space. The platform expands York’s addressable mission opportunities, drawing on its flight-proven spacecraft and established production infrastructure to meet demand for rapidly deployable, proliferated constellations at scale.

•York expanded into Geosynchronous Earth Orbit (GEO) through the U.S. Space Force’s Tetra 3 and 4 missions, referred to by York as Nemesis, broadening the company’s opportunity to support growing government demand for Space Domain Awareness missions beyond LEO. As prime contractor, York is applying its spacecraft design, sensor integration, and mission execution capabilities to these missions, with spacecraft delivery targeted for the fourth quarter of this year.

•ALL.SPACE, a wholly owned subsidiary of York, and NAVSYS demonstrated resilient communications and alternative position, navigation, and timing in a fully GPS-denied environment during the military’s Arcane Thunder exercise. Combining ALL.SPACE’s Hydra terminal with NAVSYS’s PNT-as-a-Service, the demonstration highlights the opportunity to


address defense requirements for assured connectivity and navigation in contested environments through a single, integrated platform.

•York and ATLAS, a wholly owned subsidiary of York, activated a second antenna in Mwulire, Rwanda, doubling the site’s simultaneous satellite contact capacity to meet growing demand for equatorial coverage. The addition expands capacity for existing missions and new customers, strengthening York’s global ground infrastructure and its ability to support government and commercial constellations through its integrated ecosystem.

•York identified Tomorrow.io as the customer behind its previously announced $187 million commercial contract, under which York will serve as prime contractor for the initial phase of the DeepSky early warning constellation. The program calls for dozens of satellites built on York’s M-CLASS platform.

•In October, York opened a larger Austin office to support its growing software team and development of capabilities for satellite constellations and future unmanned operations. The facility strengthens York’s ability to attract technical talent and advance the integrated systems needed to connect and coordinate distributed assets across space, land, sea, and air.

•In October, ALL.SPACE secured an order from Viasat for multiple Hydra MAX terminals, demonstrating continued commercial momentum and growing government and defense demand for resilient, multi-orbit communications. Approved to operate on Viasat’s global NextGen Ka-band network, Hydra MAX expands access to assured connectivity for mobile users while supporting York’s broader opportunity to connect crewed and unmanned platforms across contested environments.

•In November, ALL.SPACE announced follow-on U.S. Navy orders for 59 Hydra MAX 2 terminals, bringing the Navy’s total procurement to 108 systems, with combined orders valued at more than $25 million. The orders demonstrate continued customer confidence and demand for resilient, multi-orbit communications, reinforcing the opportunity to expand adoption of York’s integrated communications capabilities across crewed platforms and future unmanned fleets.

•In November, York shared that it will be working with Terma to combine York’s spacecraft platforms and manufacturing capacity with Terma’s space electronics and software expertise. The collaboration broadens York’s international opportunities, particularly in Europe, where Terma’s established relationships can open access to markets increasingly seeking sovereign space capabilities aligned with their national priorities.

•In November, Orbion, a wholly owned subsidiary of York, announced a partnership with ENPULSION to bring its flight-proven Hall-effect propulsion systems to European satellite manufacturers, expanding propulsion options and simplifying integration for a wider range of missions. The collaboration supports York’s expansion into Europe, creating opportunities for customers to draw on its broader ecosystem of spacecraft platforms, global ground infrastructure, software, and mission operations capabilities.

•In November, York announced its intent to acquire Outlook Industries, establishing the planned foundation for York Terrestrial Systems and extending its proven space, ground,


communications, and software ecosystem to unmanned platforms in the field. The proposed acquisition would open access to an established $3.08 billion unmanned ground systems market and position York to pursue opportunities in the estimated $10.31 billion unmanned surface and underwater vessel markets, supporting demand for integrated autonomous fleets while keeping warfighters farther from the fight.

About York Space Systems

York Space Systems (NYSE: YSS) is a leading, U.S.-based national defense and commercial prime providing a comprehensive suite of mission-critical solutions for national security, government, and commercial customers. York is one of the only space and defense primes with proprietary hardware and software capabilities designed to address customers’ complex mission requirements across the critical elements of the entire space ecosystem throughout the mission lifecycle. York is purpose built


to address evolving national security space challenges and to adapt to the ongoing shift in the U.S. government’s mission needs and procurement processes.

Non-GAAP Financial Measures

We believe that in addition to our results determined in accordance with U.S. generally accepted accounting principles (“GAAP”), our non-GAAP financial measures including contribution margin, contribution margin %, EBITDA, and Adjusted EBITDA provide useful information to management, investors, and analysts in assessing our financial performance and results of operations across reporting periods by excluding items we do not believe are indicative of our core operating performance. In addition to our GAAP measures, we use these non-GAAP financial measures to evaluate our operating performance, generate future operating plans, and make strategic decisions, including those relating to operating expenses and the allocation of internal resources, including budgeting for infrastructure.

These non-GAAP financial measures are used to supplement the financial information presented on a GAAP basis and should not be considered in isolation or as a substitute for the relevant GAAP measures and should be read in conjunction with information presented on a GAAP basis. Because not all companies use identical calculations, our presentation of non-GAAP measures may not be comparable to other similarly titled measures of other companies.

Non-GAAP financial measures are limited in value because they exclude certain items that may have a material impact on our reported financial results. In addition, they are subject to inherent limitations as they reflect the exercise of judgments by management about which items are adjusted to calculate our non-GAAP financial measures. We compensate for these limitations by analyzing current and future results on a GAAP basis as well as a non-GAAP basis and also by providing GAAP measures in our public disclosures.



Contribution Margin

We refer to revenue less direct material costs of revenue as “contribution margin” and contribution margin divided by revenue as “contribution margin %.” The closest comparable GAAP financial measures to contribution margin and contribution margin % are gross profit and gross profit margin %, respectively. We believe contribution margin and contribution margin % are useful measures of the variable costs that we incur in order to provide services to our customers. Our presentation of contribution margin and contribution margin % should not be construed as an inference that our future results will be unaffected by variable costs.

EBITDA and Adjusted EBITDA

We define EBITDA as net income (loss) adjusted for interest expense, interest income, income tax benefit, and depreciation and amortization. Adjusted EBITDA is defined as EBITDA adjusted for changes in the fair value of derivatives, loss on debt extinguishment, transaction costs, and other non-recurring items. Net loss is the most directly comparable GAAP measure to Adjusted EBITDA. Our presentation of EBITDA and Adjusted EBITDA should not be construed as an inference that our future results will be unaffected by unusual or non-recurring items.

For more information on our non-GAAP financial measures and a reconciliation of GAAP to non-GAAP measures, see the “Reconciliation of GAAP to Non-GAAP Results” table in this press release.

Backlog

We view backlog as a key measure of our business growth. Backlog represents our estimate of the revenue we expect to realize in future periods as a result of performing work on contracts that have been awarded to us (net of any revenue already recognized as of the backlog date). We include the aggregate expected revenue of awarded contracts in our backlog upon the execution of a legally binding agreement, even though our contracts include certain termination rights exercisable by our customers with advance notice. We exclude unexercised contract options from our backlog. Contract liabilities recognized on our consolidated balance sheets consists of payments and billings that we have received in excess of revenue that we have recognized. Because cash receipts from these contracts have not been recognized into revenue, they are included in our backlog calculation.
We monitor our backlog because we believe it is a forward-looking indicator of potential sales which can be helpful to investors in evaluating the performance of our business and identifying trends over time. Although backlog reflects business associated with contracts that are considered to be firm, terminations, amendments, or contract cancellations may occur, which could result in a reduction in our total backlog and potential future revenue that is never recognized.
